# Psychohistory resource audit — Technique 52

Status: Researching — Audit Only. Technique 51 closed at `ea102bb`, was pushed, and clean synchronized main was observed before this research. No Technique 52 engine, executable profile, CLI, simulation or benchmark was created.

The three original source ranks and four variation rows are preserved. Current publisher editions were verified separately: *Foundation* (2008 Del Rey paperback), *Social Physics* (2015 Penguin paperback, with a separately listed 2014 eBook), and *The Model Thinker* (2018 hardcover and distinct 2021 paperback). The original MIT Pentland listing's 2013 webpage date is not substituted for an edition publication date. An authorized Foundation opening excerpt was read; detailed Pentland/Page chapters and full-book algorithms remain uninspected.

Asimov's psychohistory is a fictional premise. Literary references discuss mass behavior and unawareness of the analysis; these do not establish an operational statistical law. Large synthetic populations do not validate real civilization forecasts. Pentland's author-hosted measurement/privacy context and Page's many-model context motivate careful modeling, not invented book formulas.

The strongest inspectable numerical supplement is Watts's 2002 primary threshold-cascade article, Model Specification, p5767. It uses an at-least threshold fraction, irreversible activation and random asynchronous updates. The recommendation deliberately names a synchronous finite-graph variant with exact rational thresholds. It does not claim to reproduce the paper's timing or random-graph asymptotic results.

The proposed first scope is **Synthetic Population Threshold Dynamics and Scenario Simulation — Bounded Reference Scope**: fixed binary agents, explicit graphs and thresholds, fixed or seeded initial adopters, repeated-run aggregate counts, static scenario changes, and a same-initial-state no-influence baseline. No demographic births/deaths, adaptive learning or fitted causal influence is included in that recommendation.

Original known-answer plans include:

- A three-node path at threshold 1/2 and an endpoint seed evolves through active counts 1,2,3 in synchronous rounds. A forward in-place sweep can reach 3 immediately, demonstrating different clock conventions.
- A four-node star with its center seeded and threshold 2/3 reaches 4 active nodes. Adding all missing edges gives a complete graph that stalls at 1. More links need not increase fractional-threshold adoption.
- For two linked nodes with independent initial seed probability 1/2, the four seed states give final counts [0,2,2,2]: mean 3/2 and variance 3/4. These are literal-table analytical expectations, not a simulator run.

There are 23 planned fixture families. Exact threshold equality, isolated nodes, zero thresholds, population conservation, absorbing states, update order, semantic random-number pairing and unsupported real-world claims all need explicit pre-engine fixtures. Initial seed binomial moments do not automatically describe correlated cascade outcomes.

Proposed bounds include N up to128, at most2048 edges,256 runs and8 scenarios, with an additional combined10million node/neighbor-work budget and output caps. Those maxima cannot all be combined arbitrarily. CPU and memory targets remain unmeasured acceptance proposals.

No contact files, individual traces or population data were acquired. Original synthetic inputs are recommended initially.
